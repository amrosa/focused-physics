    Worksheet Reading Quiz 01: Vectors and Kinematics
    

    Contents: This quiz covers sections 1.1 through 1.6, and you may skip material on the cross product.
    🔗
    Instructions: Answer all the questions to the best of your ability and upload your work to the "Quiz 1" assignment in Canvas.
    🔗

    1.

    The “geometric sketch” of the identity \(\vec{A}+\vec{B}=\vec{B}+\vec{A}\) on page 4 seems to assume its result: the vectors \(\vec{A}\) and \(\vec{B}\) are drawn in different order and different places. What property of vectors allows the sketch to be made as shown and keeps the proof valid?
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    On page 5, the book shows the projection of \(\vec{B}\) on \(\vec{A}\text{.}\) Draw a sketch of the projection of \(\vec{A}\) on \(\vec{B}\) and use that to establish the equations for \(\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B}\) in terms of projections.
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    On example 1.1, what trig identity is being used to relate \(\cos\phi\) and \(\cos\theta\text{?}\) Look it up if needed! How are \(\sin\phi\) and \(\sin\theta\) related?
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    On page 9, the book writes the equation \(\theta=\arctan(A_y/A_x)\text{.}\) It’s important to note we won’t always use the angle with the x axis. If we defined the angle \(\phi\) to be the angle with the y axis, with positive angle defined to be counterclockwise, then what are the components of a vector \(\vec{A}\) in terms of \(|\vec{A}|\) and \(\phi\text{?}\) Be sure to include a sketch with your answer.
    🔗

    Worksheet Reading Quiz 02: Newton’s 1st and 2nd laws
    

    Contents: This quiz covers sections 2.1 through 2.6.
    🔗
    Instructions: Answer all the questions to the best of your ability and upload your work to the "Quiz 2" assignment in Canvas.
    🔗

    1.

    The result of example 1, relating \(F_{\text{apparent}}\) to \(F_{\text{true}}\) seems to depend on the fact that Commander Wright is ahead of Commander Earhart. Draw a diagram with Earhart ahead of Wright (or Wright ahead of the fleeing ship!) and show that you get the same relationship (be careful with signs!).
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    You and a friend are on a road trip, with your friend driving. As your friend takes a sharp turn to the left, you feel yourself thrown to the right. Give an example of an inertial reference system you might want to use to analyze your motion. Identify the forces that act on you and draw a diagram representing them.
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Look at the figure accompanying problem 2.2 of K&K. This figure is slightly misleading in terms of choosing an intertial reference frame. Explain how you might change the figure or what statements you might add to make the figure physically more clear. Hint: is there an inertial coordinate system that can use the distance from the block to the pulley as a coordinate?
