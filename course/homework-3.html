    Worksheet Homework 02
    

    Objectives

    The purpose of the homework is to:
    🔗
    • Practice applying the concepts and techniques discussed this week
      🔗
      🔗
    • Reveal gaps in your understanding so you know what to study
      🔗
      🔗
    • Enable a productive re-read of the textbook chapter/engagement with other course resources.
      🔗
      🔗
    The homework is both a valuable learning opportunity and a gauge of how well you’re doing. Did you solve all the problems by yourself with no help? Incredible! If that didn’t happen, that’s OK: we’re not expected to get everything right the first time. What you need to do is look carefully at what you needed help with, and then make a study plan, which will likely include solving more problems, to fill in the gaps in your understanding.
    🔗
    🔗
    Instructions: Complete all the exercises below and submit your work by the due date.
    🔗

    1. A Special Collision.

    Two objects of equal mass are approaching one another with equal speeds v and collide head-on. By the symmetry of the situation, after the collision their velocities reverse. Using only this information, determine what happens to the objects after a different collision: one of them is at rest, and the other one, initially with speed \(v\text{,}\) collides head-on with it.
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Crossing the river.

    For the swimmer problem we did in class, consider the alternative scenario: what if instead of trying to cross the bank in the shortest time, we are trying to cross the bank by swimming the shortest distance? When the swim speed \(V\) is larger than the current speed \(u\text{,}\) we solved this in class. What should you do when \(v\lt u\text{?}\) There’s an elegant solution, but if you go about it by trying to directly find the minimum of the distance, try to extremize the simplest quantity.
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Catching the bus.

    You woke up late and are worried about missing the bus. As you approach the bus stop, you spot the bus at a distance \(a\) directly west of the bus stop. You are at a distance \(b\) directly south of the bus stop, but you’re walking through a park and can run in any direction. If the bus drives at speed \(V\text{,}\) what is the minimum speed \(u\) at which you need to run to catch the bus?
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Using Vectors to Describe Motion.

    A wheel of radius \(R\) is spinning clockwise on a fixed axis with a constant angular velocity \(\omega\text{.}\) Coordinate axes are chosen as shown, and the axis of the spinning wheel travels with uniform velocity \(\vec{V}_0\) along the x axis. The value of \(V_0\) is an independent parameter, and is not related to \(\omega\) in any way. For example, the axis of the wheel is attached to a train car, traveling with that velocity, and the wheel just happens to be spinning at some rate.
    🔗
    Imagine painting a particular point on the wheel in red. At t=0, the axis of the wheel is at \((0,R)\) and point you painted is at \((0,0)\) (“the ground”)
    🔗
    
    A cross section of a circular wheel in the x y plane. The center of the wheel lies along the x axis, and it is moving to the right with speed \(V_0\)
    🔗
    A clockwise curved arrow labels the angular speed \(\omega\text{,}\) and a point on the wheel is shown as a full circle.
    🔗

    (a) Warm-up: motion of a point on the wheel.

    Find the position, velocity and acceleration vectors for the point, as a function of time.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b) Rolling without slipping.

    If our point on the wheel were to be moving horizontally whenever it touched the ground, there would be a lot of friction between the wheels and the track, which would lead to wear on the wheels. A real wheel often satisfies the no-slip condition, meaning that the point of the wheel touching the ground is never moving horizontally.
    🔗

    (i)

    Show that this condition is satisfied if \(V_0=\omega R\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (ii)

    Qualitatively explain why there is no friction between the wheel and the ground only when this condition is satisfied.
    🔗
    🔗

    (iii)

    Write expressions for the position, velocity, and acceleration vectors of the point on the wheel in terms of \(\omega, R, t\) only.
    🔗
    🔗

    (iv)

    What is the acceleration of the point on the moment it touches the ground?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c) A Common Confusion.

    The relationships (1) \(v = \omega R\) of the speed of a particle going around a circle, and (2) \(V_0 = \omega R\) of the previous part look very similar but describe two completely different things. Don’t confuse them!
    🔗
    To illustrate the difference, consider the following scenario. A motorcyclist revs up the engine, kicks into gear, the wheel spins up almost instantaneously, and the motorcycle races off in a puff of smoke, leaving the smell of burnt rubber and a big black mark on the asphalt. Let \(\omega (t)\) denote the angular speed of the tire.
    🔗

    (i)

    Which speed in this problem (of what, relative to what?) is related to \(\omega\) by relationship (1), and which by relationship (2)?
    🔗
    🔗

    (ii)

    Note that of these two relationships, one holds throughout the scene described, and the other only for a portion of the time! Comment which is which.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
